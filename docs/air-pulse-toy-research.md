# Air-Pulse Toy Research – How Womanizer, Satisfyer & Co. Make Their Pulses

*Compiled 2026-11-16 for the V-Contour clitoral air-pulse pulser (a second 12 V diaphragm pump, valves removed,
on a T at the clitoral port). Companion to [clitoral-chamber-sizing-research.md](clitoral-chamber-sizing-research.md)
and [cup-cleaning-and-entrapment-safety.md](cup-cleaning-and-entrapment-safety.md).*

## Why this exists

The plan is to copy the air-pulse effect of commercial toys **on top of** the clitoral chamber's steady engorgement
vacuum. A diaphragm pump moves the same amount of air every stroke, so turning its motor up makes the pulses
**faster but not much stronger**. The question was: do the toys change strength, speed, or both – and what does
that mean for our pulser?

**Short answer:** the toys treat **strength (stroke size)** and **speed (pulse rate)** as separate things. Womanizer's
own patent lists "the size of the deflection, the frequency, the modulation" as what the controller sets, and the
Womanizer Next has 14 speed levels plus a separate 3-level "depth" control. To copy them properly, the pulser needs a
way to set strength on its own.

## 1. How the toys work (from their patents)

### Womanizer – Novoluto GmbH, inventor Michael Lenke
Patent family DE 10 2013 110 501 / WO 2015/039787 / US 9,849,061 / US 9,937,097 / US 10,857,063 ("Stimulation device").

- **Two chambers joined by a short passage.** A *first chamber* inside the toy has a flexible wall. A *second chamber*
  (the nozzle) sits over the clitoris. A **motor turns an eccentric (or a connecting rod)** that pushes and pulls the
  flexible wall, so the first chamber's volume goes up and down.
- Bigger first chamber → **negative pressure** (suction) in the nozzle; smaller → **positive pressure**. The pressure
  swings around room pressure.
- Other drives named: a **piston** (can squeeze the chamber to "zero or almost zero"), a **piezo bending element**,
  hydraulic, pneumatic and electromagnetic drives.
- **The pressure generator can be remote:** one version puts the drive in a separate unit joined to the nozzle by a
  hose that "may be 0.5 m in length". (Same idea as our pulser on the machine with a tube to the cup.)
- **What the controller sets:** "the size of the deflection, the frequency, the modulation, etc." – i.e. **stroke and
  rate separately**, plus the pattern.
- **Example patterns:** pulsed high-amplitude, pulsed low-amplitude, irregular (uneven in timing and strength), and a
  pressure that **increases over time** to follow arousal. One example starts with a **long steady negative pressure
  ("3 minutes for example")** and then switches to pulses of slowly rising strength – the same "engorge first, then
  pulse" plan as the V-Contour.
- **Limits:** negative pressure "preferably less than the usual systolic blood pressure"; maximum pressure limited by
  the size of the parts so as to minimise risk of injury. No numbers for frequency, stroke, volume or pressure are
  given in the text read.
- **Intensity also depends on** the nozzle chamber's size relative to the displaced volume, and how close the opening
  sits to the skin.

### Satisfyer – EIS GmbH
EP 3 228 297 B1 ("Pressure waves massage apparatus", priority 2016; Google Patents lists it as revoked).

- **One cavity** (no separate second chamber), closed at the back by a **flexible silicone membrane**; the opening is a
  removable silicone spout over the clitoris.
- **Motor + eccentric or connecting rod** moves the membrane back and forth; roughly **sinusoidal** pressure.
- **Key sizing rule (claim 1): volume change ÷ smallest cavity volume between 1/10 and 1;** preferred **1/8 to 1/2**.
  In other words the membrane stroke moves **one-eighth to one-half of the whole cavity volume** every cycle.
- Pressure is mainly **suction pulses**: any overpressure should be **no more than 10 %** of the suction amplitude.
- Intensity: stored "modulation patterns" picked with a button; the text read does not say whether levels change
  stroke or speed.

### Newer designs
- **US 2024/0398656 A1** ("Non-Contact Induction Air Wave Massage Device"): a **magnetic piston sliding in a rigid
  cylinder**, driven by a coil, linear motor or rotating magnet – "no flexible wall or membrane is required".
  Alternates under- and over-pressure.
- **LELO "SenSonic"** (Sona): marketed as "sonic waves" through a suction nozzle; no technical details found.
- **Satisfyer Pro 2 Gen 3 "Liquid Air"**: two motors (one for pressure waves, one for vibration), two caps; mechanism
  not published.

## 2. Pulse rates

| Device | Pulse rate | Source quality |
|---|---|---|
| Womanizer Premium 2 | **minimum about 20 pulses per second** | Womanizer launch material, repeated by reviewers |
| Womanizer Next ("3D Pleasure Air") | **as low as 3 pulses per second** | Womanizer launch material |
| Womanizer (general) | "50–240 Hz depending on the level" | comparison blog, **no source or method – unverified** |
| Satisfyer (general) | "40–200 Hz depending on the model" | same blog, **unverified** |

**No independent measurement** (pressure sensor or oscilloscope at the nozzle) of any air-pulse toy was found.
The best numbers will come from **measuring your own toy**: a phone spectrum-analyzer app (Spectroid, phyphox) near
the toy gives the motor hum, which roughly equals the pulse rate; a pressure sensor on a sealed nozzle sampled at
≥ 1 kHz gives the real pulse rate and strength.

## 3. Intensity levels – strength vs speed

| Device | Levels | What a level changes |
|---|---|---|
| Womanizer Next | 14 levels + 3 "Climax Control" depth levels | Reviews describe the 14 levels as **speed** and Climax Control as the **depth of the air waves** – two separate controls |
| Womanizer Premium 2 / Pro / Classic 2 | 14 / 11 / 10 | not stated |
| Satisfyer Pro 2 (all generations) | 11 | not stated |

Reviewers describe Womanizer as "thuddy" and Satisfyer as "buzzy", especially at high settings – consistent with
Womanizer using a bigger, slower stroke and Satisfyer a faster, smaller one, but this is subjective.

**Conclusion:** at least Womanizer separates **rate** from **depth/stroke**, and its patent lists stroke size as a
controlled setting. A pulser that can only change motor speed covers half of what the toys do.

## 4. What the nerves respond to

- **Qi et al., *Nature* 630:926–934 (2024)** – Krause corpuscles in the **mouse** clitoris and penis are fast-adapting,
  light-touch vibration sensors. They respond best to vibration at **40–80 Hz** and follow it up to 120 Hz (the
  highest tested). The clitoris has a higher density of them than the penis. Activating them caused vaginal
  contraction in females. *Mouse data – not proven for humans.*
- **Human skin in general (not genital):** Meissner corpuscles are most sensitive at low frequencies (roughly 3–40 /
  below 50 Hz); Pacinian corpuscles have their lowest threshold around 150–200 Hz.

So the useful range is probably **a few pulses per second (slow, "deep") up to around 100 Hz**, with the 40–80 Hz band
worth special attention.

## 5. Clinical evidence (thin)

- **EROS-CTD** (FDA, 2000) – gentle clitoral vacuum for arousal disorder; see [vulva-pump-research.md](vulva-pump-research.md).
- **Alexander et al., *Arch Phys Med Rehabil* 99(2):299–305 (2018)** – 12-week randomized trial, clitoral vacuum
  suction device vs vibrator in women with MS or spinal cord injury (~23 completed). Both helped; authors suggest the
  vibrator first for cost, and **the vacuum device for women with several sexual problems or when vibration doesn't
  work**.
- Pilot ultrasound study (single volunteer) of clitoral blood flow and size after a wand, an internal vibrator and an
  air-pulse toy – blood flow/size only, not orgasm.
- A Womanizer pilot in women around menopause (designed by Dr Jennifer Berman) is reported only in a press release.

## 6. What this means for the V-Contour pulser

1. **Strength needs its own control.** A diaphragm pump's stroke is fixed, so add one of:
   - a **"softener" side volume** on the pulser line that can be switched in or out (more air volume = weaker pulses),
     e.g. one or two small valves to step strength;
   - a pulser with an **adjustable stroke** (adjustable crank/cam);
   - a **voice-coil (speaker) pulser**, where strength and rate are both set electrically.
2. **Our chamber is much bigger than a toy nozzle.** The Satisfyer patent moves 1/8–1/2 of the cavity volume per
   stroke. cup_020's clitoral chamber is ~34 cm³ plus tubing; the spare pumps probably move only ~0.3–2 cm³ per turn,
   i.e. about **1/100 to 1/17 – below the patent's minimum of 1/10**. Expect weaker pulses than a toy unless:
   - the engorged clitoris fills much of the chamber (it does reduce the air volume), and/or
   - the pulser moves more air per stroke (bigger diaphragm/piston), and/or
   - the air volume between pulser and clitoris is kept as small as possible (short, narrow, stiff tube; restrictor
     on the vacuum line).
   **Measure before deciding** (sealed test jar or plate + fast pressure reading).
3. **Rate range to support:** from ~3 pulses/s (Womanizer Next "deep" style) to ~100 Hz. A diaphragm pump at
   2,000–5,000 RPM gives 33–83 Hz, right on the 40–80 Hz band. Very slow pulses (3–20 per second) may be easier as
   **bursts** (switching the pulser on and off) than by running the motor very slowly under vacuum load.
4. **Keep it suction-only.** The toys mostly pulse *suction* (Satisfyer: overpressure ≤ 10 % of the suction swing).
   With the steady engorgement vacuum underneath, our pulses never go positive – no air is pushed into the body.
5. **The "engorge, then pulse" plan matches the Womanizer patent**, which describes a long steady suction phase before
   pulsing with rising strength.
6. **Safety stays the same:** steady vacuum + pulse peak ≤ **75 mmHg** (the toys only say "below systolic blood
   pressure", ~120 mmHg; our cap is stricter). Peak pressure has to be checked fast (≥ 1 kHz), not by the 100 Hz
   control loop.

## What was not found

- Measured pulse rates or pressures for any commercial toy (only manufacturer claims and one unsourced blog).
- Chamber volumes, stroke lengths or nozzle sizes in numbers (patents give ratios only).
- A peer-reviewed study of air-pulse toys and orgasm.
